What this is

A real plan for a bed I have not seen

This is the same packet I make for a client, built for a bed that exists in a lot of towns around here. A 16 by 4 ft strip against something, in full sun or sun until mid-afternoon, in a place where deer walk through at night. If that is your bed, this plan will work in it. If it is not quite your bed, the notes below say what changes.

What I can promise: every plant here is hardy in zone 6b, rarely or never browsed, sold by a nursery you can drive to or a grower who ships, and chosen for a reason I have written down. What I cannot know: your soil, your exact light, and how bad your deer are. The custom version starts with a visit, and that is the whole difference between $25 and $250.

Why the cottage border. It is the most searched garden look in America this year, and almost none of it survives a Connecticut deer. Roses, hostas, tulips, phlox: gone by July. What is left is the discipline underneath the abundance, and that turns out to be the part that matters.
If your bed is in shade, stop here. Under six hours of sun the peony and salvia flop and the coneflower sulks. The Woodland Edge Edit, coming in January, is the one for you.
The look

Abundance on a strict grid

The romance works because the bones are hard. A straight front edge you keep crisp, a wall behind, and one thing going up in the middle. Everything loose sits inside that frame.

Something goes up. A cottage border always has a vertical. Here it is a clematis on a 6 ft obelisk at the centre back, because the rose the archetype wants is deer food.

The colour stays warm and soft. Blush, dusty rose, cream, silver leaf, and one plum note so it never goes sweet. Nothing primary.

The middle does the work. Drifts of five and seven carry June to September. Fewer kinds, bigger groups, so it reads from the lawn and not just from the path.

It is honest about winter. This border rests from November to March. What holds it is the hydrangea heads, the coneflower seedheads, the obelisk, and the geranium's red winter leaf along the front.

The plants

Eight plants and two bulbs

Tap a photo to enlarge. These are stand-ins from Wikimedia Commons until I photograph my own.

1. dwarf panicle hydrangea Hydrangea paniculata 'Bobo' · 36 in tall · 2 plants
White cones in July that blush pink by September and dry to parchment for the winter. Three feet, no staking, and the one shrub that gives a border its bones without a hedge. Blooms Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct. Deer: rarely. Feeds: bees on the fertile florets.

2. clematis Clematis 'Betty Corning' on an obelisk · 84 in tall · 1 plants
Lilac-blue bells the size of a thimble from June to September, lightly scented, climbing the obelisk at the centre of the bed. Cut it to a foot in March and it does it again. Blooms Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep. Deer: rarely. Feeds: a little; this one is for you.

3. peony Paeonia 'Sarah Bernhardt' · 36 in tall · 2 plants
The blush double peony from every June wedding, and it lives fifty years. Deer never touch it. Give it a ring support in May or the rain will lay it down. Blooms Jun. Deer: never. Feeds: ants, not much else; the honest one.

4. meadow sage Salvia nemorosa 'Caradonna' · 24 in tall · 5 plants
Plum-purple spikes on black stems in June, the one saturated note in the bed. Shear after flowering and it comes back in August. Bees work it the whole time. Blooms Jun, Aug, Sep. Deer: never. Feeds: bees, heavily.

5. purple coneflower Echinacea purpurea 'Magnus' · 30 in tall · 3 plants
Rose-pink daisies from July into September, then dark seedheads the goldfinches strip in October. Leave them standing for the winter picture. Blooms Jul, Aug, Sep. Deer: occasionally. Feeds: bees and butterflies; goldfinches on the seed.

6. aromatic aster Symphyotrichum oblongifolium 'Raydon's Favorite' · 30 in tall · 2 plants
A lavender-blue dome that opens in October when nearly everything else is done, and smells of balsam when you brush it. The last nectar of the year. Blooms Oct. Deer: occasionally. Feeds: late bees and monarchs on the way south.

7. catmint Nepeta 'Cat's Pajamas' · 14 in tall · 6 plants
Grey-green mounds with lavender spikes from May to frost, spilling over the front edge the way a cottage border should. Shear once in July. Deer and rabbits leave it alone. Blooms May, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep. Deer: never. Feeds: bees, all season.

8. cranesbill Geranium x cantabrigiense 'Karmina' · 10 in tall · 6 plants
A tidy front-edge mat, pink in May and June, leaves that go red in November and hold through winter. It keeps the edge clean while the middle is loud. Blooms May, Jun. Deer: never. Feeds: small bees.

b. Allium 'Purple Sensation' 25 bulbs
Four-inch violet spheres on 30 in stems in May, in fives between the peonies. The plum note before the salvia takes over. Deer never touch them.

b. Narcissus 'Thalia' 40 bulbs
Cream, not yellow, two or three nodding flowers a stem, woven through the middle in April. If it sells out, 'Starlight Sensation' is the same job with more flowers per bulb.

The honest paragraph. Two of the eight are native, the coneflower and the aster. The rest are well-behaved non-natives that earn their place: the catmint and salvia are among the heaviest-worked bee plants in any garden, and the peony and clematis are for you. If you want the native share higher, swap the catmint for short-toothed mountain mint and give it a ring of edging, because it spreads.

Care

Four sessions a year

  1. March. Cut everything to the ground except the hydrangea (to 12 in) and the geranium (leave it). Clematis to 12 in. Two inches of compost, no fertiliser. An hour.
  2. May. Peony rings on before the buds get heavy. In the first year, water weekly and deep from now to September. After that, only in a drought.
  3. July. Shear the catmint and the salvia by half once the first flush is over. Both come back. Deadhead the peonies.
  4. November. Nothing. Leave the seedheads and the hydrangea heads. If you like it tidier, cut the coneflower and aster to 6 in and leave the rest.

Where control lets go. Nowhere, by default. If you want the cottage looseness, let three Verbena bonariensis seed at the front edge and pull what you do not want in May.
