Why trust it

Tested at home first

The first client was my own garden. Every rule in my process was learned in my beds in Greenwich before it ever touched a client's: which "deer-proof" plants actually are, how narrow a bed can run before the plan has to change, what a care plan honestly costs in hours. When I hand you a packet, it's the same one I hand myself.

You work directly with me, not a project manager, and not an algorithm deciding your plants. Behind me is an AI studio I built myself. It does the checking: every plant against your sun, soil, and browse pressure, every palette against a panel of design standards. I do the choosing.

And this practice is of this region, not shipping into it. I know which "deer-resistant" plants actually get eaten in Pound Ridge, which inkberry cultivar dodges the boxwood blight running through Fairfield County, and which nursery on your own road stocks the plants in your plan.

The standards

No random acts of gardening

01

Deer, rabbits, and wet feet are design inputs

If it gets eaten in your town, it isn't in your plan.

02

No dead months

Something blooms, and something is fed, every month from April to October.

03

Native-forward, never dogmatic

The test is whether a plant belongs in this region, not where its ancestors lived. If a well-behaved non-native earns its place, it's in.

04

Drifts, repetition, clean edges

That's what makes natural look deliberate instead of neglected.

05

Maintenance honesty

The care plan tells you the real annual cost in hours before you plant, not after.
